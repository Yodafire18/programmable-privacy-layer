# Vision

**[Name] lets you describe, in plain English, what your camera should never capture, test it, then wear it.**

## How the idea evolved
Finding the story was our biggest challenge. Even the three of us saw it differently, and everyone we showed it to had a different opinion. That told us it shouldn't be one filter. **It should be a programmable privacy layer.**

## The magic: MCP
Because of MCP, you can use natural language to set the rules, in ways that are beautiful and testable.

> Blur faces, but let people who show two peace signs unblur, so they can consent to be photographed.

## Build it, test it, wear it
1. **Describe it.** A doctor using a Meta Quest talks to our MCP, which runs the logic on the computer: *"Blur documents, X-rays and children's faces."*
2. **Test it.** The profile is tested with synthetic data.
3. **Tune it.** They try it and adjust it.
4. **Wear it.** They use the profile from glasses or anything else.

## Taking it to the next level with MCP
- **Natural language to profile.** Say what to blur and what to allow; the MCP turns it into a custom profile.
- **Tested with synthetic data.** Profiles are tested on synthetic data.
- **Tune and reuse.** Adjust the profile, then use it from glasses or any other device.

## Who it's for
- **Vloggers who livestream their lives:** filter out bystanders and license plates.
- **Doctors:** blur documents, X-rays and children's faces.
- **Streamers:** cover up the brands of drinks they don't like.
